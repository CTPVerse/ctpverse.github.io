##### What the numbers say

- Goldman's $1.8T prices the existing pillar — orbital launch, satellites, communications, defense programs, space-derived data. It assumes chemical propulsion. It contains none of CTP's pillar: crewed deep-space transport, deep-space cargo & logistics, government deep-space exploration contracts, commercial crewed expeditions. As a reference point for a new pillar, $1.8T is a floor, not a ceiling.
- The market has already paid $1.77T for the pinnacle of the old paradigm. One chemical-rocket company's IPO valued it at roughly 98% of Goldman's entire 2035 industry number (first-day close ≈$161, ≈$2.1T market cap; above $2.1T by early October 2026). CTP's base case — $1.05T enterprise value at year 10 — sits below what public markets already paid for the old paradigm. The scenario economics read as measured against the market's own price tag, not as fantasy.
- Goldman documents the last paradigm shift: $65,400 per kilogram to low Earth orbit in 1981 → ~$1,500/kg today — a 98% collapse that turned space from a government program into an institutional asset class ($55B+ invested in 2025; a record $36B in Q1 2026 alone). The $1.8T is priced on that physics. Gravitic propulsion is the next order-of-magnitude shift in the cost of movement — and every previous shift built an economy the old model could not price.
- Musk's four-word response to Goldman's forecast: “It will be much bigger.”

#### Part C — The nth Trillion

### Interstellar civilization

Parts A and B of this document are priced — Part A in the market's own third-party numbers, Part B in the v3 model's scenario economics. This part is deliberately not priced, and that is the honest statement.

No dollar figure is attached to the Star Drive markets, because humanity has never achieved interstellar civilization. There is no historical base rate to model from and no prior shift at this scale to extrapolate. Any specific number would be invention. The projection is therefore stated as the nth trillion: the scale is open-ended by definition.

Under near-instantaneous FTL travel regardless of distance, the accessible economy becomes the universe itself.

##### What changes

- **Distance ceases to be the binding constraint.** Every market in history has been priced by the cost of crossing distance. Remove that cost and the addressable market is no longer a planet or a solar system — it is every reachable star system, then every reachable galaxy.
- **Resource extraction scales to stellar inventories.** Mining is no longer limited to one planet's crust.
- **Settlement compresses to generational time.** Human settlements on other worlds within a single generation become a logistics problem, not a physics problem.
- **Commerce and transit-as-a-service extend to interstellar, then intergalactic range.**

Each previous expansion of the reachable economy — the Age of Sail, rail, aviation, orbital launch — created markets the prior model could not price. The Star Drive is that transition at civilizational scale: a completely different world, a civilizational paradigm. The whole universe becomes the addressable market.

nth trillion. Unlimited.
